# Addendum 4: Regular Admins Are Sealed Out of the Sharing Graph (Super-Admins Unchanged)

> **STATUS: MERGED & IMPLEMENTED — Jul 16 2026.** Everything below is now folded into `permissions-model.md` and built in `backend.py`, `frontend.js`, and the backend/browser regression suites. Storage remains unchanged. No normative implementation amendments were needed: regular-admin relationship keys are omitted with `sharing_visible: false`; superadmins retain the full surface with `sharing_visible: true`; all four admin collaborator routes gate on superadmin before map existence. The browser harness ignores Chromium's network-isolation resource warnings while still failing on application console errors and uncaught exceptions. Do not re-apply; kept for the design rationale.

> **Scope of this addendum in one line:** everything below changes the **regular-admin** experience only. **Super-admins are untouched** — they keep full share management (writes, per-map rosters, the shared-with list, and the owned-list rosters) exactly as today, subject only to the pre-existing no-self-grant guard.

## Motivation

Regular admins can currently reach into the map-sharing graph in ways that belong to the map's *owner*, not to whoever manages the owner's account. Two distinct problems, one privacy and one authority:

1. **Authority overreach — mutating other people's collaborations.** The base spec's "Edit user's map shares" permission lets a regular admin **un-share** (sever a collaboration two users deliberately created) and **share on someone's behalf** (disclose an owner's map to a third party the owner never chose). Both are the admin substituting their judgment for the owner's about the owner's own map. A `controls()` grant is about **managing accounts** (enable/disable, reset password, edit profile) — not about arbitrating who collaborates with whom.

   **This is also a live bug, not just a design smell.** Condition 1 gates the write on *control of the map's owner* and Condition 2 leaves the *target* unscoped — so an admin who controls owner **X** can remove **any** collaborator from X's map, **including a super-admin or a user the admin does not control** (`admin_delete_map_share` → `_guard_admin_map` → `_guard_admin_target(owner)` never checks the removed party; `_remove_user_share` is a bare `DELETE … WHERE map_id=? AND user_id=?`). This is exactly the un-share overreach above, reachable today. Removing the regular-admin write path **fixes this bug structurally** — there is no counterparty-scoping patch to write, because there is no write.

2. **Relationship privacy — a share is a connection between two people.** A share links two specific people: the plain fact "**X shared a map with Y**". Independent of any map *contents*, that fact — *who is connected to whom* — can itself be sensitive. (We call this a **connection** throughout; it's the who-shared-with-whom link, as distinct from the map's contents, which were already sealed.) The base spec's admin map views expose these connections in both directions: the *shared-with* list reveals whom a controlled user **receives** maps from, and the *owned* list's `collaborators` roster reveals whom a controlled user **shares out to** — assembled across every user the admin controls into a de-facto picture of who collaborates with whom, and including other people **outside the admin's scope** (super-admins, other admins' users).

   Contents were already sealed (metadata-only lists). But the base spec litigated *contents* privacy and never named *relationship* privacy — that is the gap this addendum closes. **This tool is being released for open/unknown deployment**, where a share may be mundane org structure or may be highly sensitive. We design for the sensitive case: who is connected to whom is **not** the regular-admin's business, and admins have no DB access that would let them reconstruct it anyway.

Both problems trace to wiring the control hierarchy into the sharing graph. This addendum severs that wiring for regular admins — which is the spirit of **Invariant 2** (map sharing is a graph independent of the control hierarchy) taken to completion.

## The change, in one sentence

**A `controls()` grant no longer lets a regular admin see or change *another user's* map-share connections — who that user's maps are shared with, in either direction: no share-write, no per-map roster, no shared-with list, and no collaborator rosters on the controlled user's owned maps. Through the admin console they see a controlled user's maps only as bare metadata (name, date, marker/photo counts). Super-admins keep everything.** (Regular admins retain share-*group* operations on groups they own — membership curation and the base spec's creator-scoped delete — a deliberately-kept surface that does not breach this seal; see "Why share-group operations stay" below, including its two named residuals.)

**Scope note (read the title this way):** "sealed out of the sharing graph" is shorthand for *the sealing of one specific edge* — the authority a `controls()` grant used to confer over **another user's** shares. A regular admin remains an ordinary participant in the sharing graph **for their own account**: they still own maps, manage the shares on maps they personally own (owner routes, `is_map_owner`), and see maps shared *to* them. Those owner-facing paths are deliberately untouched (see "Implementation plan → owner-facing share routes"). The seal is on *control-derived* access to *other people's* connections — not on the admin's own ordinary ownership. Do not read the title as license to tighten the owner-facing routes.

## Why "no list" is a feature, not a hidden bug

An earlier draft worried that hiding a deliberately-made share "reads as a bug" (a real share missing from a list of other shares looks broken). That objection only bites when the share is missing *from a list that shows other shares* — the **inconsistency** is what reads as a defect. Remove the list category entirely and there is no baseline to contradict: "the admin panel does not surface received shares" is a stable, explainable product boundary, exactly like "the admin panel does not show map contents." A capability that was never advertised generates no bug reports. So dropping the shared-with list — rather than trying to show it "consistently" — is the clean cut, and it is a deliberate feature of the model, not an omission.

Note explicitly what this does **not** claim: it is **not** a secrecy guarantee against a determined admin. The sanctioned loud path — reset the controlled user's password and log in as them — still reaches that user's own view (including the maps and shares that user can see). This addendum removes an admin's ability to **mutate** sharing and to **passively assemble** the picture of who is connected to whom from the admin console; it does not, and cannot, promise "your admin can never observe this exists." The honest model is: **no write, no console-side view of who-shares-with-whom, impersonation remains the loud exception.**

**Be precise about what "loud" buys you — today it means *disruptive*, not *attributable*.** Password-reset impersonation is loud in the sense that it **locks the real user out** (their password stops working), so it is eventually *noticeable* by the victim. It is **not** durably *attributable*: there is **no audit log anywhere in the system** (see §5), so nothing records *that* the reset happened, *who* did it, or *when* — the trail is "the user notices they're locked out and complains," not a tamper-evident record. So the seal is honest for the deployments it targets (open/unknown, where you want no passive console assembly and no routine mutation) but it is **not** isolation from a *hostile* account administrator: such an admin can still reset-and-impersonate, and until audit logging exists (a *proposed, not-yet-built* audit-log design lives at `logging-system.md` — a separate work item), even that loud path leaves no attributable record. For a genuinely hostile-admin deployment two things are needed beyond this addendum: (a) **audit logging**, so the loud path is also *traceable*; and (b) **user-driven password recovery**, so an admin cannot *choose* a controlled user's credentials at all. Neither is in scope here; this addendum is the passive-inspection + routine-mutation seal, stated without overclaiming.

## Why sealing regular admins but not super-admins is principled

Super-admins are exempt **because of their role, not their infrastructure access.** They are the model's unscoped actor (Invariant 5): every cross-scope and destructive action already escalates to them, and their share management is the operational-recovery / pressure-release valve that keeps routine fixes (remove a stale collaborator, repair a broken share) from forcing impersonation. That justification is deployment-independent — it holds however the system is hosted and whoever holds the super-admin role.

It is *additionally* true, in the common single-operator deployment, that the super-admin is also the person with database access, so their UI hides nothing they couldn't `SELECT` directly — hiding relationship metadata from them would be theater. But that is a supporting observation, not the reason: the exemption follows from the unscoped role, and stands even for a super-admin who has the app role without shell credentials. (Under this addendum's own "design for the sensitive, unknown deployment" premise, we must not lean on assumed infrastructure access — hence the role, not the DB, carries the argument.)

## Why share-group operations stay (and don't breach the seal)

Regular admins keep membership add/remove and roster visibility on **share groups they own** — "What does NOT change" leaves all group operations untouched. This is not a leftover hole in the seal, and the distinction from one-to-one map shares is structural, not cosmetic:

- **The write is doubly scoped.** A membership change requires `controls(admin, user)` **AND** `owns_share_group(admin, group)` — both the admin and the user they add lie inside the admin's delegated reach, and no out-of-scope person is ever named in the operation. The removed map-share path was the opposite: Condition 1 gated only on control-of-*owner* and never scoped the *other party*, which is exactly what let an admin remove an out-of-scope collaborator (Motivation §1). That structural defect simply cannot arise on the group surface — so the "recreates the authority concern indirectly" worry is answered by the scoping itself, before any consent argument.
- **Out-of-scope effects ride on delegation, not overreach.** Adding controlled user X to owned group G does change which out-of-scope owners' maps X can reach — but only through maps shared *to that group*, and sharing *to a group* is by definition consenting to a curator-managed recipient set: the owner chose "whoever the group's curators include, now and later" when they picked a group over a named person. Membership churn is inside that delegation. A one-to-one share carries no such delegation — the owner chose one specific person — which is precisely why arbitrating *those* connections had to leave admin hands while curating group composition does not.
- **Rosters are curation, not relationship surveillance.** Group membership is administratively authored — only admins/superadmins ever add a member; a user never self-joins (Invariant 1: share-group membership is administratively inert). So Motivation §2's relationship-privacy argument does not attach — a shared group membership shows an *administrative grouping*, not a connection two users chose to make. And the owning admin sees only their group's *composition* (who is in it); which maps are actually shared to the group stays sealed, so the roster discloses no real map-share connection.
- **Named residual (accepted):** an owning admin sees — and per the base rules cannot remove — out-of-scope members a superadmin placed in their group. This is unavoidable (you cannot curate a list you cannot see) and discloses administrative grouping only, not any map-share connection.
- **Second named residual (accepted) — creator delete severs invisible inbound edges.** The base spec's creator-scoped share-group delete survives this addendum, and deleting a group cascades away every map→group share (`map_group_shares` is `ON DELETE CASCADE` on the group FK) — including shares from owners entirely outside the creator's scope, which under this addendum the creator cannot see (inflow is sealed), so they delete blind. This is deliberately accepted, not gated, for three reasons. (a) The delegation argument extends to lifecycle: an owner who shares *to a group* consents to a curator-managed recipient set **and** to the group's curator-managed existence — a group that can be curated can also cease to exist; an owner who wants a durable share names a person instead. (b) Gating delete on out-of-scope inbound shares would leak the sealed fact through the 403 itself — that at least one inbound connection exists, and that its owner is out-of-scope. Unlike the member-scope gate (whose 403 discloses nothing the visible roster doesn't already show), an inbound-share gate would be the one place in the sealed model where inflow existence seeps out. (c) Because share targets are global, any user could freeze any group against deletion just by sharing a map to it — a veto held by people the creator can't see, producing deletes that fail for reasons the error must either hide (baffling) or state (leaking). Membership scope therefore stays the only delete gate, unchanged from the base spec.

## What changes (regular admins only)

### 1. No share-write path
The regular-admin half of "Edit user's map shares" is **removed entirely.** Conditions 1 and 2 no longer apply to regular admins — they do not edit shares at all. This closes the un-share bug in Motivation §1. The self-dealing exception is moot for them (there is no write to guard).

Read "no share-write path" precisely: it means no ***map-share*** write path. The share-group surface a regular admin keeps — membership curation, and the base spec's creator-scoped group delete, which cascades that group's `map_group_shares` rows — does write to the sharing graph's group edges. Both are argued and bounded in "Why share-group operations stay" above, including the delete residual; they are named exceptions, not holes.

### 2. No per-map collaborator detail
The per-map endpoint `/admin/maps/<id>/collaborators` (GET as well as PUT/DELETE) becomes **super-admin-only**. GET returns the map's *full collaborator roster* — every recipient's identity — which is strictly more relationship data than a regular admin should hold. Note precisely where the leak is: today `_guard_admin_map` already restricts a regular admin to maps whose **owner** they control (it 403s otherwise), so the concern is **not** reaching maps owned outside the admin's scope. The concern is that even on a *controlled* owner's map, the roster names **recipients** — collaborators the admin has no authority over — which is exactly the who-shares-with-whom connection §2 seals. A regular admin has no read path to this endpoint at all.

### 3. No shared-with list
The "**Shared with this user**" section of the admin map view is **not rendered for regular admins**, and the payload **carries no shared-with data** for them. A regular admin never sees which maps a controlled user *receives* from others. (See "Why 'no list' is a feature" above.)

**JSON contract (pin this exactly and test it) — an explicit visibility signal, not a silent empty array.** An earlier draft kept the `shared` and `collaborators` keys as empty arrays `[]` "for backward compatibility." That was **rejected**: `[]` is a *semantic* lie. Every existing consumer reads `"shared": []` as "this user receives no shares" and `"collaborators": []` as "this map has no recipients" — but the truth is "you are not permitted to know." Syntactic key-presence is not compatibility when the value now means the opposite of what it used to. Worse, it directly contradicts this addendum's own "Why 'no list' is a feature" argument (an empty entry sitting where real data used to be is exactly the misleading baseline we set out to remove). So for a regular admin the payload:

- **Omits the `shared` key entirely** (top level) — there is no shared-with data and no empty stand-in for it.
- **Omits the `collaborators` key** from every owned-map row (§4) — the row carries `id`, `name`, `created`, `is_base`, `markers`, `photos` and nothing about other people.
- **Adds a top-level `"sharing_visible": false`** flag, so a consumer can positively distinguish "sealed from this caller" from "genuinely empty" without guessing from a missing key.

For a **super-admin** the relationship data and UI behavior are unchanged — `shared` and `collaborators` are present and populated exactly as today — and the payload **gains one additive field, `"sharing_visible": true`**. (This is the one sense in which "super-admins unchanged in every respect," used throughout this addendum, is about *authorization and visible behavior*, not byte-for-byte JSON: a super-admin sees and can do exactly what they could before; the response simply carries one new, purely informational boolean.) So the contract is: *presence of the relationship keys tracks `sharing_visible`* — both are present together for a super-admin, both absent for a regular admin, and the boolean names the reason. "Sealed, and honestly labeled so" — not "present but empty," and not "silently missing."

### 4. No collaborator roster on owned maps
The owned-map rows drop their `collaborators` roster for regular admins. An owned-map row becomes **name / date / marker count / photo count** — no other people named. This seals the *outbound* connection (whom the controlled user shares *to*), symmetric with §3 sealing the *inbound* connection (whom they receive *from*). Both directions of "who is connected to whom" are gone from the regular-admin console. **Wire contract:** the `collaborators` key is **omitted** from each owned-map row for a regular admin (not present-but-empty) — see the visibility-signal contract in §3; the row's other keys (`id`, `name`, `created`, `is_base`, `markers`, `photos`) are unchanged.

### 5. Super-admins: unchanged in every respect
Super-admins keep the write path (PUT/DELETE), the per-map roster GET, the shared-with list, and the owned-map collaborator rosters — the Administration screen behaves for them exactly as it does today. **The no-self-grant guard is retained for super-admins** (base spec line 138): a super-admin still cannot add *themselves* as a recipient of a map, nor a share group they belong to. This is the one guardrail that survives, and it applies to the super-admin *map-share write path*.

Scope of that guard, stated honestly: it makes the **direct** self-grant — the quiet, targeted "add myself to this one map" — impossible, which is the covert path worth blocking. It is **not** an absolute "an administrator can never reach content quietly" guarantee, and this addendum does not claim one. The base spec already records the **accepted asymmetry** (line 138): a super-admin *may* add themselves to a share *group* as a member, indirectly reaching every map shared to that group. That path is deliberately left open and is not equivalent to the direct self-grant — it is over-broad (you get the whole group's inflow, not one chosen map) and **roster-visible while it lasts** (the super-admin's name sits in a membership list the group's owner/co-owners can see). Precise on how visible: it is *potentially observable* — a co-owner who looks at the roster can notice it — but **not durably traceable**: there is no audit log anywhere in the system, and a super-admin can remove their own membership again afterward, leaving no record. And in the degenerate case of a group the super-admin solely owns, no other owner exists to observe the roster at all. So it is *louder than the direct self-grant* (which leaves only a `map_shares` row a co-owner would never see) but it is not a strong tamper-evident trail. The guard is *correct for the direct path*, and the group-membership route is the base spec's named, accepted exception — not a hole this addendum closes. **If durable traceability of that exception is ever wanted, it requires adding audit logging (which does not exist today); this addendum does not add it.** The sanctioned, *loud* content-access path for administrators remains password-reset impersonation.

## Updated rule text (drop-in replacements)

**Replace the "Edit user's map shares" row in the Admin Permissions table with:**

> **Edit user's map shares** — **Super-admin only.** A super-admin may edit any map's share list (unscoped, per the global-target rule below), subject to the no-self-grant guard. Regular admins have **no** share-write path. An admin who genuinely must change a user's sharing uses the loud, sanctioned path — reset the user's password and act as the user.

**Replace the "View user's maps (owned + shared)" row with:**

> **View user's maps** — the *target* differs by caller role and is unchanged from today:
> - **Regular admin:** `controls(admin, user)` **and** the target's role must be `"user"` only — never another admin or a super-admin, and never the admin themselves (an admin's own maps live in the app). The view is **bare metadata only: map name, date, marker count, photo count** (plus the row's `id` / `is_base` flags) — no collaborator rosters and no "shared with this user" section. Both directions of the sharing graph (whom the user shares out to, and whom the user receives from) are sealed, because a share is a relationship between two people and arbitrating/observing that graph is not an account-manager's role (Invariant 2).
> - **Super-admin:** unscoped (Invariant 5) — may view **any existing account**, including admin-level accounts and themselves, and sees the **full view**: owned-map rosters, the shared-with list, and the per-map collaborator detail. Unchanged from today.
>
> *(The role/self-view restriction is the regular-admin rule, not a global one — the super-admin path is deliberately unrestricted here, matching `_guard_admin_target`, which returns immediately for a super-admin, and the existing "super → admin: 200" test. Applying "user-only / no self-view" to both roles would be a new, unintended behavioral change and contradicts "super-admins unchanged in every respect.")*

**Replace the "Edit user's map shares — the two conditions" section** entirely. The old two-condition structure was built around a regular admin (Condition 1 = `controls(admin, map_owner)`, Condition 2 = target is global); after this addendum the regular-admin path is gone, so Condition 1 cannot survive as written — an unscoped super-admin has **no** `controls()` requirement. Drop the paired-condition framing and the regular-admin worked example (Admin-A can/cannot edit — no longer reachable) and replace with a flat super-admin rule:

> **Editing map shares (super-admin only).**
> - **Who:** super-admins only, via the `/admin/maps/<id>/collaborators` routes. There is **no `controls()` requirement** — a super-admin may edit the share list of **any** map (Invariant 5, unscoped actor). Regular admins have no share-write path at all.
> - **Targets:** global / unscoped — a share may name any user or any share group (management groups are never share targets).
> - **The one guard (no-self-grant):** a super-admin may not grant *themselves* access — neither a direct self-recipient add nor a grant to a share group they belong to. (The base spec's accepted asymmetry stands: a super-admin *may* add themselves to a share **group's membership** separately, a broader but roster-visible path — see decision §5. That is not a share-edit and is not closed here.)

**Restate Invariant 2** so it reflects the severed wiring. The base spec's Invariant 2 gates editing shares on control over the map's owner; after this addendum **no** administrative share action uses `controls()` — owners manage their own shares, and super-admins act under their unscoped authority. Replace the base Invariant 2 text with:

> **Invariant 2 — Map sharing is independent of the control hierarchy.** Map *owners* manage their own shares (owner routes, gated by `is_map_owner`). Super-admins may manage any map's shares as the explicit **unscoped** exception (Invariant 5), subject to the no-self-grant guard. `controls()` grants a **regular admin neither read nor write** access to any map-share connection — not the per-map roster, not the shared-with list, not the write path. Share *targets* remain global (a share may name any user or share group), and management groups are never share targets.

This is a *tightening*, not a reversal: the base invariant already declared sharing an independent graph; wiring `controls()` into the admin share surface was the deviation, and this restatement removes it. (Consequently, the "Invariants 1–5 hold as-is" line in *What does NOT change* is scoped to 1, 3, 4; Invariants 2 and 5 are restated, not bent — Invariant 5 for the "cross-scope *governance*" clarification below — see that section.)

**Amend the Visibility section (base spec lines 96–104)** to add a regular-admin sealing bullet:

> - A regular admin's view of a controlled user's maps is **bare metadata** — name, date, marker/photo counts only. It carries **no collaborator roster** (whom the user shares out to) and **no "shared with this user" list** (whom the user receives from): the map-sharing graph is a relationship between people and is sealed from regular admins, consistent with Invariant 2. There is no read-only version of it either — the category simply does not exist in the regular-admin console. **Super-admins are exempt** (they see full rosters and the shared-with list) because they are the model's unscoped actor (Invariant 5) — the exemption follows from the role, not from assumed infrastructure access (that a single-operator super-admin also has DB access is a supporting observation, not the reason).

The existing bullet — *"The map-sharing pickers (`/users`, `/groups`) stay deliberately global for every authenticated user"* — is **unchanged and load-bearing**: seeing that user Y *exists* in the picker is not the same as seeing that X *shares with* Y. The sensitive fact is the connection (who shares with whom), not the mere existence of a person. Do not "tighten" the picker in a later pass — it would break sharing (the super-admin global-target rule depends on it) for no relationship-privacy gain.

**Clarify Invariant 5's "cross-scope" (base spec line 167).** As written, Invariant 5 says superadmins are the *only* actors who can perform "cross-scope actions" — but a regular admin curating an **owned** share group's membership has an indirect cross-scope *effect* (an added member gains reach to maps owned by out-of-scope owners who shared *to* that group). That is not a violation, and this addendum's "Why share-group operations stay" section depends on it not being one. Narrow the invariant to prohibit cross-scope *governance* (authority reaching outside the admin's scope), not incidental effects of in-scope actions. Replace the base Invariant 5 text with:

> 5. **Superadmins are the only actors who can perform destructive/irreversible or cross-scope *governance*.** "Cross-scope" here means an administrative action whose *authority* reaches outside the acting admin's control scope — not any action with an indirect cross-scope *effect*. Superadmin-only governance covers: hard delete (user, or any group beyond the creator-scoped share-group case in the Delete row), delegation/revocation of `controls()`, `owns_share_group()` and `manage_mgmt_group()` themselves, and every management-group operation of any kind (create, rename, delete, membership). **Not** covered: a regular admin curating an owned share group's membership, which *incidentally* changes which out-of-scope owners' maps the added member can reach. That is an in-scope action (both admin and member lie inside the admin's reach, and no out-of-scope party is named) whose cross-scope reach rides on the owner's prior delegation — sharing *to a group* is consenting to a curator-managed recipient set (see "Why share-group operations stay" above). The invariant prohibits governing outside your scope; it does not prohibit in-scope actions that have effects outside it.

This is a *clarification*, not a loosening: the behavior it describes (regular admins curate owned groups; membership churn has delegated cross-scope reach) already exists under Addenda 1–3 — the base wording just didn't distinguish governance from effect. Applying it here keeps it in the same atomic merge as the rest of Addendum 4, so the base spec is never left in a state where Invariant 5 is revised while regular-admin share editing is still permitted elsewhere.

## What does NOT change

> **Audit follow-up — Jul 16 2026:** this section described the Addendum-4-only merge.
> The later audit feature adds an append-only audit table, endpoint, and tab but does
> not change Addendum 4's authorization or relationship-visibility rules.

- **Super-admins** — full share management, per-map rosters, shared-with list, owned-map rosters, and the retained no-self-grant guard. Their Administration screen is unchanged.
- **Ownership and account-management permissions** — enable/disable, reset password, edit profile, create/delete/assign users, all group operations — untouched. This addendum is scoped to map *share* visibility and mutation only.
- **Contents stay sealed.** No spot, photo, description, or note data is added to any payload; regular-admin map rows in fact carry *less* now (no rosters). Password-reset impersonation is unchanged and remains the only **sanctioned, loud** content-access path — and for a **regular admin** it is the only path at all (they have no share-write and cannot self-add to a group they don't own within scope). It is **not** literally the *only* way a *super-admin* can reach contents: the base spec's accepted share-group self-add (§5) is a second, quieter-but-roster-visible route that this addendum deliberately leaves open. "Only way" is therefore scoped to regular admins and to the sanctioned fallback — not an absolute claim about super-admins.
- **The map-sharing pickers stay global** for every authenticated user — that a person *exists* is not the sensitive fact; the connection (who shares with whom) is. Do not scope the pickers.
- **The `/admin/users` list keeps exposing each user's share-*group* memberships** (the detail pane's "Share Groups" line; `backend.py` builds this from `share_group_members` across **all** groups, not only groups the caller owns). This is deliberately **out of scope** for this addendum and consistent with it: a group membership is **administrative grouping**, not a map-share connection (Invariant 1 — membership is administratively inert; a user never self-joins), and it discloses no who-shared-with-whom fact — *which* maps are shared to a group stays sealed. It is called out here only so the seal's boundary is explicit: this addendum removes map-share *connections* from the regular-admin console, not share-group *membership* labels. (If group-membership visibility should itself be scoped to owned groups, that is a separate change, not this one.)
- **Invariants 1, 3, 4** hold as-is. **Invariants 2 and 5** are *restated* (not bent). **Invariant 2** — its base text gated share-editing on control over the map's owner, which no admin path uses after this addendum; the drop-in above rewrites it to say `controls()` grants regular admins neither read nor write access to any share connection. **Invariant 5** — its base "cross-scope actions" wording is narrowed to cross-scope *governance*, so a regular admin curating an owned share group's membership (an in-scope action with a delegated, incidental cross-scope *effect*) is correctly not a violation. Both *tighten* alignment with each invariant's own premise (sharing is an independent graph; superadmins govern outside-scope, they don't merely cause outside-scope effects) rather than changing the model. No new invariant is needed.
- **Storage** — no tables, columns, or migrations. `controls_predicate()` and all control/ownership relations are untouched.

## Rejected alternatives (for this addendum)

- **Option A — keep regular-admin share management, just show super-admin-owned shares read-only.** Rejected: leaves the *authority* overreach in place (regular admins could still un-share or share-on-behalf, incl. the live bug in Motivation §1) and keeps the visibility/mutability coupling. Removing the write path is simpler and more correct.
- **Option B — let regular admins manage shares of super-admin-owned maps too.** Rejected: would require rewriting Condition 1 and carving exceptions into Invariants 4 and 5, for a capability the role shouldn't have.
- **Show the shared-with list read-only (an earlier draft of *this* addendum).** Rejected once relationship privacy was named: a read-only list still discloses the connections — whom a controlled user collaborates with, including people outside the admin's scope — assembled across the admin's whole scope. "Metadata-only" sealed *contents*, not *relationships*. For an unknown/sensitive deployment, who-is-connected-to-whom is itself the thing to protect.
- **Scoped middle — show a share only when the admin also controls the other party (redact the rest).** Rejected: (a) it still leaks by inference ("shared with someone outside my scope" itself reveals that a connection exists); (b) more code for a weaker guarantee than simply not rendering the list; (c) it revives the "deliberate share looks like a bug" tension for the hidden rows. Dropping the category outright is both simpler and strictly more private.
- **Counterparty-scoping the write instead of removing it** (let a regular admin remove a share only if they also control the removed party). Rejected: contradicts Condition 2's "targets are global," only half-fixes (an admin could still un-share between two users they both control — still arbitrating others' collaborations), and keeps admins in the sharing-mutation business we're removing them from. Removing the write path fixes the bug with no new scoping logic.
- **Privacy-framed absolute — "hide the map from the admin entirely so two users can share in secret."** Rejected as an unbackable promise: the impersonation path means an admin can always reach a controlled user's own view. The honest model is no write + no console-side relationship view, with impersonation as the acknowledged loud exception — which is this addendum.
- **Stripping super-admins of share management too.** Rejected: it would break Invariant 5's unscoped-actor role and force impersonation for routine share fixes — super-admin share management is the pressure-release valve. (That a single-operator super-admin also has DB access, making UI-hiding theater, is a supporting point, not the load-bearing reason — see "Why sealing regular admins but not super-admins is principled".) The no-self-grant guard is the correct constraint on the super-admin **direct** map-share write; it is not claimed to make all administrator content access loud, since the base spec's accepted share-group self-add asymmetry remains (a broader, roster-visible path — see decision §5).
